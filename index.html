The case

The smarter path we're pioneering,
versus traditional rockets.

A rocket is a machine for surviving a launch. A spaceliner is a machine for carrying people. Those are different problems — so we stopped solving the first one.

01 — Speed & comfort

You will not be pinned to your seat.

Gentle, sustained acceleration along an aerodynamic climb profile, not a vertical shove. The kind of ride your body already knows.

  • Runway takeoff, like any aircraft
  • No G-forces that pin you back
  • Smooth, unpowered glide home
02 — Accessibility

It leaves from an airport, not an island.

If a runway can take a widebody, it can take a spaceliner. That collapses the distance between a passenger and a launch to a taxi ride.

  • Departs from major spaceports
  • No remote launch pads
  • Airline-inspired operational cycle
03 — Sustainability

Nothing is thrown into the ocean.

Expendable hardware is what makes spaceflight rare and expensive. A vehicle that flies again this afternoon changes the entire economic shape of orbit.

  • Fully reusable airframe
  • Lower emissions per seat
  • Multiple flights per vehicle, per day

The traditional rocket

  • Vertical launch from a remote pad
  • 3–4 g sustained, peaking higher
  • Stages discarded or recovered by sea
  • Weather and range windows measured in days
  • Crew training required to fly
  • Turnaround measured in weeks
Versus

The Providence spaceliner

  • Horizontal takeoff from an existing runway
  • An acceleration profile built around passengers
  • One vehicle, returned intact, every flight
  • Departure windows measured in hours
  • You board it the way you board a jet
  • Turnaround targeted in hours

Flight profile

Five phases. One vehicle.
Same day, if we do this right.

The planned operational sequence, from the gate to the gate.

Phase 01

Ground operations

Rapid vehicle preparation and diagnostics at a conventional gate. Passengers board from a jet bridge.

Status — Workflow modelling
Phase 02

Assisted / hybrid launch

Horizontal takeoff under airbreathing power, transitioning to the hybrid cycle as the air thins.

Status — Simulation phase
Phase 03

High-altitude transition

Autonomous trajectory management through the region where wings stop working and thrust takes over.

Status — Guidance validation
Phase 04

Orbital / suborbital operations

Passenger-safe environmental systems hold the cabin at conditions a person can simply sit in.

Status — Architecture development
Phase 05

Autonomous recovery & landing

A silent, unpowered glide onto a runway — the vehicle returned intact and ready to be turned around.

Status — Recovery logic testing

Technology

The science behind the dream —
in active development.

Multi-mode propulsion

Research focused on operational efficiency, controllability and reusable flight cycles — a propulsion system judged on how often it can fly again, not only on how hard it can push.

Digital engineering

Digital twins, structural simulation and model-based systems engineering to cut iteration cycles and catch integration failures in software rather than in hardware.

Design for manufacture

Transportation systems are viable only if they can be produced, maintained and operated at scale. Manufacturability is a design input here, not a downstream problem.

“Safety is treated as a primary architecture constraint — not a downstream feature.”

Safety & reliability

What we validate, and how.

Rigorous iterative validation is the foundation — wind tunnels, structural analysis, redundant systems, and operational resilience from day one.

  • Structural analysis
  • Thermal stress evaluation
  • Failure-mode simulations
  • Redundant systems architecture
  • Flight software verification
  • Operational risk modelling

Roadmap

Our path to the stars.

A phased development strategy emphasising safety, manufacturability and operational scalability. We share every major milestone, because this endeavour belongs to all of us.

2026Phase 1

Foundation & systems architecture

Establish the engineering, organisational and digital backbone.

  • Core systems engineering architecture
  • Vehicle conceptual configuration studies
  • Propulsion trade studies
  • Aerospace digital workflow setup
2026–2028Phase 2

Digital engineering & prototype development

Transition from concept to engineering-grade prototype systems.

  • Digital twin development
  • Structural simulations
  • Avionics and GNC architecture
  • Prototype propulsion subsystem testing
  • Scaled aerodynamic validation
2028–2031Phase 3

Propulsion & flight demonstration

Develop reusable propulsion systems and validate flight operations.

  • Hybrid propulsion integration
  • Hypersonic systems research
  • Autonomous flight software
  • Flight envelope expansion
  • Reusable thermal systems
2031–2035Phase 4

Commercial operations preparation

Build airline-compatible operational capability.

  • Certification pathway development
  • Fleet maintenance architecture
  • Predictive maintenance systems
  • Spaceport operational planning
  • Airline partnership integration
2035+Phase 5

High-capacity space transportation

Deployment of reusable, high-frequency spaceline operations.

  • Large-capacity reusable spaceplanes
  • Point-to-point hypersonic transportation
  • Orbital transportation systems
  • Global spaceline ecosystem
  • Integrated propulsion service network
